07 · Business model

Free for people. Paid by those who want them to stay.

HOSTS AND VENUES

First-timers become regulars

Partner host tools and return-rate reports, in aggregate only. Referral fees on ticketed events. [$__ / month]

INSTITUTIONS

Help your people land

Universities, co-op programs, employers who relocate staff, newcomer services. [$__ per seat]

ATTENDEES, LATER

Firefly Plus

Optional extras, such as [a new-city mode]. The feed, pods and picks stay free.

Never for sale: your data, your going list, or your attention.

Proposed revenue lines, not yet validated with customers.

LinkedIn showed a social network can be a venture-scale business when the people using it are free and the organisations that want access to them pay, through several lines at once. Firefly’s version: hosts and venues pay to turn first-timers into regulars (the reports are aggregates only; hosts never see picks or individual choices). Institutions pay to help their people settle: a university for international students, a co-op office, an employer relocating staff, a settlement agency. Later, an optional Plus for attendees. What we won’t do: ads, selling data, or dark patterns like streaks and “someone liked you” upsells. That’s a trust position, and in a product about meeting strangers, trust is the moat. All of this is a hypothesis to test with real organizers and institutions; fill the prices once you have conversations behind them.
