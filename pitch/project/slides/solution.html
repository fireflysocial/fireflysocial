Design concept. Names and events are made up.

The aha in one look: Firefly is useful to one person on day one, before any network exists, and gets social as more people go to the same events. That’s how it avoids the cold-start problem every social app dies of. Walk the phone: onboarding takes under two minutes and ends on the feed, so value comes first. Each card says why it’s there. “I’m going” is self-reported after you register on the event’s own page, because we never see other sites’ guest lists. Every confirmed “I’m going” teaches Firefly who will be where, which is exactly what pods need next. Pods are 3–5 people because spontaneous conversation tends to cap around four (Dunbar, 1995), and random seat neighbours at an intro session were more likely to be friends a year later (Back et al., 2008). Casual friendship takes roughly 40–60 hours together (Hall, 2019): one event can’t do that, meeting again can. The feed is the launch product and is being built now; pods and picks are designed (connection-flow draft: pods form by each person's intent for the event), not built. This phone is a design concept: replace it with a real screenshot or a live demo when you have one. Safety is built in: no location data ever, minimal profiles for strangers, one-tap block and report.
