# 222

**Category:** Planned meetups (curated small-group outings)
**Last researched:** 2026-11-15

## What it is

A subscription app (founder Keyan Kazemian) that matches strangers into small groups for curated outings (dinners, bars, activities). It began as backyard dinners for friends and strangers. It's in ~14 cities including NYC, LA, London, and **Toronto**.

## What it does

| Area | Details |
|------|---------|
| Matching | Onboarding survey of ~30+ questions sorts users into one of 16 personality categories. A profile is built and a group is picked for each event. Questions include income, orientation, and college major. |
| Learning | Post-event feedback is used to tune future matches. The founders suggest attending at least 5 events before judging it. |
| Intent | Users are split roughly 50/50 between friendship and "open to romance." |
| Price | ~US$22/month subscription. |

## Strengths

- Iterative matching: it improves from feedback, which one-off dinner apps don't do.
- Curated venues and experiences make it feel like a night out, not a chore.

## Gaps (relative to Firefly's goal)

1. **Mixed intent.** The 50/50 friendship and romance split blurs signals. People looking for friends sit with people looking for dates.
2. **Intrusive onboarding.** Asking about income and orientation is a trust barrier and a data liability.
3. **Algorithm misses.** Reviewers report "high compatibility" predictions with people they didn't click with at all.
4. **Same one-shot problem.** Every outing is a new group by default, and repeated contact isn't the design goal.
5. **Not in Vancouver** (as of research date).

## What Firefly should take from it

- **Feedback-trained matching** is a feature to adopt: each event's "who did you click with" data should improve the next grouping.
- Be explicit that Firefly is **platonic**. Mixed intent is a known failure mode.
- Collect the minimum. Don't ask for income or orientation.
