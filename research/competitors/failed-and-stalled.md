# Failed & Stalled Friendship Products

**Purpose:** answer the research question "Why did previous friendship apps fail or stall?" ([research/README.md](../README.md)).
**Last researched:** 2026-11-15

| Product | What it was | What happened | Likely reason |
|---------|-------------|---------------|---------------|
| **Hey! VINA** | "Tinder for girlfriends": swipe-based women's friend matching (2016) | Shut down after ~10 years. No public reason given. | Chat-first matching with no shared context. Hard to make money from platonic intent. |
| **Geneva** | Group chat and community app | Bought by Bumble (2024), then shut down and merged into BFF (2025). **$0 revenue** as of June 2025. | Communities grew, but there was no business model for them. |
| **Bumble For Friends (standalone)** | Spun out of Bumble (2023) | Relaunched within 2 years as BFF on Geneva's stack. Parent cut ~30% of staff. | 1:1 swipe matches stalled in chat. Pivoted to groups. |
| **leap.club** (India) | Women's professional community | Halted operations, citing funding crunch and **retention challenges**. | Community value didn't hold month to month. |
| **Dinner Lab** | Pop-up supper club with strangers | Shut down (2016) after failing to find a sustainable business model. | Event operations cost too much for the margins. |
| **Lunchclub** | AI-matched 1:1 professional video meetings | Pivoted to video during COVID and faded. No confirmed shutdown date found. | Professional focus; once the novelty faded, people stopped coming back. |
| **Meetup under WeWork** | Interest groups | 2019 RSVP-fee test caused an organizer revolt. Activity declined, and it was sold twice. | Charging the supply side (organizers) drove them away. |

## Patterns

1. **Retention is the core problem, not acquisition.** "Getting people to download is easy; getting them to use it more than once is hard" (TNW, Mar 2026). Friendship has less urgency than dating, so users don't push through friction.
2. **Chat-first loses to event-first.** Every 1:1 matching app ended up adding groups or events.
3. **Success removes the need for the app.** Once you have friends, you leave. This is the "churn when it works" problem. Revenue has to come from somewhere other than lonely-user subscriptions alone.
4. **Charging organizers backfires** unless the product clearly earns them money or attendance.
5. **Running events yourself is expensive.** Companies that operate their own events (Dinner Lab, Timeleft, 222) carry heavy costs. Layering on top of existing events is lighter.
