# Event Networking Apps (Brella, Swapcard, Grip)

**Category:** Professional event networking
**Reviewed:** 2026-11-15

These vendors offer event networking and meeting tools. This category is relevant to host-supported introductions, but professional networking differs from friendship.

Brella's own help documents [intent-based matchmaking](https://help-organizers.brella.io/en/articles/177659-how-matchmaking-works) and [numbered meeting points](https://help-organizers.brella.io/en/articles/182746-tables-meeting-points). These support the specific meeting-point precedent, not a claim about friendship outcomes.

## Evidence limits

The original notes relied on vendor comparisons and reviews: [Swapcard versus Brella](https://www.swapcard.com/compare/swapcard-vs-brella), [Swapcard versus Grip](https://www.swapcard.com/compare/swapcard-vs-grip), [Swapcard engagement](https://www.swapcard.com/features/attendee-engagement-software), and a [Brella review](https://creatoreconomytools.com/tool/brella). Several pages timed out during this audit. Vendor comparisons are not independent evaluations.

The material does not establish that host promotion is the only route to adoption, that half of attendees get no value when half use a tool, or that all contact and directory access end with the event. Current contracts, features, access duration, and pricing need direct verification. Untraced “nearly 100% adoption” and “3x more meetings” claims are not usable benchmarks.

## Implications for Firefly

Test registration-time invitations, explicit consent to introductions, and a fixed meeting point. These are design hypotheses, not proven friendship outcomes. Organizer-paid software is a model to investigate, not evidence that the economics work for volunteer clubs.
