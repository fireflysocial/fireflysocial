# Inspiration / Problem

In September 2026, I moved from Victoria to Vancouver for an eight month software engineering co-op. With seven times the population and far more diversity, I assumed my social life would naturally expand. Instead, making genuine friends proved harder than ever.

When I asked a friend who has lived here for two years how she builds meaningful connections, she said: <b><i>"Once you find out, let me know."</i></b>

She is not an outlier. Metro Vancouver's struggle with social isolation, known locally as the "Vancouver Freeze," is well documented, and the pattern behind it is easy to recognize. People here are often described as polite and pleasant in everyday interactions, yet reluctant to move from acquaintance to close friend. Many residents, especially those who grew up in the city, already have friend groups that date back to high school or university, and they have little reason to make room for someone new. Even when a connection seems promising, it often ends with a vague "we should hang out sometime" that never becomes an actual plan.

The city itself makes this worse. High living costs make casual outings expensive, and long workweeks leave little free time. Months of grey, rainy winter keep people indoors, and much of social life has moved onto phones and group chats. For someone actively trying to make friends, every friendly conversation that goes nowhere wears down their motivation to try again.

Run clubs, events, and social apps exist, but they rarely bridge the gap between a first hello and a real friendship. Coordinating is too much effort, so people default to staying home.

Firefly doesn't wait for people to find each other. It brings together like-minded people who genuinely want to connect, and keeps them meeting until acquaintances become friends and friends become a community.
