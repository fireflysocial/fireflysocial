## Key numbers

**2025: Survey on Social Connections – Well-being in Canada** (target population: residents aged 15+ in the 10 provinces; fieldwork Apr 11 – Jun 22, 2025; sample size not given here; excludes territories, full-time institutional residents, and residents of First Nations reserves and settlements)

| Contact with friends in the past month | Share | Always/often lonely | Satisfied with friendships |
|---|---|---|---|
| Both virtual and in person | 88% | **15%** | **87%** |
| Virtual only | 10% | **24%** | **63%** |
| In person only | 2% | — | — |

- **Recent immigrants** (within 10 years) are twice as likely as non-immigrants to have **virtual-only contact with friends in the preceding month**: **16% vs. 8%** (established immigrants 13%).
- Black (16%) and South Asian (15%) Canadians are more likely to be virtual-only than non-racialized, non-Indigenous Canadians (9%).
- StatCan notes these associations "do not imply causation."

**Youth loneliness (always or often), ages 15–24**
- 2021: **23%** (all ages: 13%)
- Q1 2024: **17%** (see [science.md](../science.md))

These come from different survey waves, so treat the drop as indicative only.

## Takeaways

- **In-person contact is associated with lower reported loneliness:** 24% in the virtual-only group versus 15% in the mixed-contact group. These are different groups, not a before/after reduction or evidence that Firefly will cause an improvement.
- **Recent immigrants more often reported virtual-only contact.** Virtual includes telephone contact, not just online communication. Friends left behind are one possible explanation, not a measured location of those friends or proof of greatest need.
- Young people remain the loneliest age group nationally, matching the Vancouver Foundation's local finding ([vancouver-foundation-surveys.md](vancouver-foundation-surveys.md)).
