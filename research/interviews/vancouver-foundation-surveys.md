## Key numbers

**2012**
- ✓ **One-third** say "It is difficult to make new friends here."
- ✓ **One in four (25%)** agree: "I find myself alone more often than I would like to be."
- ✓ **50%** of people in Canada for fewer than five years, and **44%** of people in their neighbourhood for fewer than five years, say it is hard to make new friends.
- ✓ **25–34-year-olds:** **41%** say making new friends is difficult (vs. 24% of people 65+), and **33%** feel alone more often than they'd like.
- The report's background describes isolation as a leading concern in a separate 2011 consultation with community leaders; do not attribute that ranking to all respondents in the 2012 resident survey.

**2017**
- ✓ **Around half** "continue to find it difficult to make friends."
- ✓ About **one in four** still find themselves alone more often than they would like.
- ✓ **14%** feel lonely "often or almost always." The report calls this "lower than expected."
- ✓ Spending time alone more often than they'd like: **23%** of all residents, **31%** of 18–24s, **47%** of households under $20K.
- ✓ About **half** expect to move in the next five years, or are unsure.
- ✓ Frequent loneliness: **30% of 18–24-year-olds** and **38% of respondents in households earning under $20K**. These are separate groups, not an age-by-income subgroup (2017 report, printed p. 9 / PDF page 11).
- ✓ Difficulty making new friends: **60% of 18–24-year-olds** and **67% of 25–34-year-olds** (same page).

## Quotes

> "Young people… they spend more time alone than they would like, and they experience loneliness 'almost always' or 'often.'" — **Kevin McCort**, Vancouver Foundation president (Global News, 2017)

> "The reasons that people aren't participating are largely related to time and money." — **Kevin McCort** (Global News, 2017)

## Correction note

The earlier version of this repository incorrectly rejected the **67%** figure. The 2017 report explicitly gives that figure for difficulty making new friends among **25–34-year-olds** (printed p. 9 / PDF page 11). The **41%** figure is from the separate 2012 report. Both belong to their respective surveys; neither should replace the other.

## Comparability

The 2012 survey combined telephone and online responses; 2017 was online only. The 2017 report uses only the 2012 online subset for its comparisons. Do not interpret the full 2012 one-third figure versus the 2017 "around half" summary as a measured increase. The 2017 authors also caution that results may not generalize to the entire adult regional population.

## Takeaways

- Difficulty making friends is measured; these surveys do not establish a uniquely Vancouver phenomenon or a ranking against other cities. **Young adults and newcomers are hardest hit**: half of recent immigrants find it hard to make friends.
- **Time and money** are the stated barriers. Time alone more than wanted roughly doubles from all residents (23%) to low-income households (47%).
- **Housing uncertainty:** about half expected to move **or were unsure**. This does not mean half planned to move, nor does the survey establish that moving caused friendship loss.
- Caveat: the newest wave is from 2017, and no newer Vancouver Foundation survey was found as of November 2026. For recent national data, see [statcan-friendship-surveys.md](statcan-friendship-surveys.md).
