**Method:** desk research from a convenience sample of 15 source notes, not 15 independent interviews. Sources sometimes repeat the same interviews or surveys. The 2026-11-15 audit checked accessible sources and marked blocked material in each note; quotations retained from earlier notes are not all independently reverified. ## Recurring themes

Grouped by theme. No formal coding or independent-source frequency count was performed; repeated coverage is not independent corroboration.

1. **Follow-through may be a bottleneck** (CBC, Zetta, Zada/Beattie, Reddit, We Should Be Friends, Lumi). Zetta's reported 305 contacts and two regular contacts illustrate one experience, not a general conversion rate. People swap numbers, then nobody follows up: *"People don't call you back, people don't invite you out."*
2. **Closed circles and a "leave me alone" norm** (CBC/Sofia Katrina, BCIT/Lee and Wong, Myles/Juliette, Zada, Narcity). Locals already have friends from high school or university. People are polite but don't *invite* others in: *"'I'll leave you alone—if you leave me alone' is the overall vibe."*
3. **Time, money, and exhaustion** (Vancouver Foundation, BCIT, Namoro/Esme, Myles/Daniel, Zetta). $15–20 per hangout, paycheque-to-paycheque living, 6–7 night workweeks. Time alone more than wanted rises from 23% of all residents to 47% of households under $20K.
4. **Reaching out feels risky** (Anna Ho: "like a job interview," Reddit: "got ghosted both times"). These accounts suggest rejection can discourage further attempts.
5. **Recurring, accessible formats feature in several positive accounts** (Girls Who Walk, Mondays Run Club, book club → "auntie to a baby," Lumi's "little by little," UBC roommates). Several accounts describe **repeated contact**; selection bias prevents a claim that this is necessary or sufficient.
6. **Shared intent may reduce awkwardness** (We Should Be Friends: "everyone else here came alone too," Namoro: "everyone is going with the same intention," Reddit: wary of strangers "until they ask for something").
7. **Newcomers can face particular barriers, but it isn't only them.** In the 2012 Metro Vancouver survey, 50% of respondents in Canada for under five years found it hard to make friends. In the 2025 national survey, recent immigrants (within ten years) were twice as likely as non-immigrants to report virtual-only friend contact in the past month. These are different populations and definitions. Lifelong locals (Anna Ho, Esme) and long-time residents ("lived here for years and still had no friends") feel it too.
8. **Founders are former lonely newcomers.** Lumi, We Should Be Friends, Mondays Run Club, and VanCity Girls Who Walk were all started by people who moved here and couldn't find friends. They are natural host partners (now listed in [organizers.md](../organizers.md)).

**Counter-evidence (keep it honest):** some people say it's a myth if you put in effort (Narcity OP, Dylan Wharndy: "I'll meet friends at the bar"). A friendship coach warns the "freeze" label can become an excuse. Dr. Joey Moore (Douglas College) says it's "not just a Vancouver problem." Cluny South (KPU) notes that cities with a lot of newcomer flux tend to be *more* open. The 2017 survey found loneliness "lower than expected" (14% often or almost always), even though about half find it hard to make friends.

## Implications for the workflow

Mapped to [v1-user-flow.md](../../workflow/v1-user-flow.md) (v2 draft):

| Finding | Flow step | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Follow-through is the bottleneck (theme 1) | **6 Second meeting** | Motivation to test step 6, not validation of it. The "same time next week" default matches Martin's "plan within two weeks" rule. Consider tightening the **21-day fallback** (no shared event) to **14 days**. |
| Ghosting makes people quit (theme 4) | 5 After, 7 Ongoing | Keep non-mutual picks invisible (already planned). Also nudge the person who **hasn't replied** in a new chat, while allowing people to decline or stop replying without pressure; no design can guarantee an absence of perceived rejection. |
| Closed circles (theme 2) | 3 Before (pods) | Pods give newcomers a ready-made circle instead of asking them to break into one. At first meetings, weight pods toward **similar newness**. |
| Fear of rejection (theme 4) | 0 Onboarding, 4 At event | Test shared intent. A truthful host introduction is **"everyone in this pod opted in to meeting people."** Do not assume everyone arrived alone. |
| Cost (theme 3) | 0 budget preference, 1 Discover | Keep the budget filter and **rank free events first** for "free" users ([open-gaps #16](../../workflow/open-gaps.md)). |
| Recurring formats work (theme 5) | 1 Discover | Supports "recurring series ranked first." Big clubs (150–200 people) are great supply but need pods so people **find the same people again**. That is Firefly's pitch to hosts ([open-gaps #9](../../workflow/open-gaps.md)). |
| In-person contact is linked to lower loneliness (StatCan 2025) | Overall | Motivates testing in-person contact; does not causally establish superiority over chat-first ([failed-and-stalled](../competitors/failed-and-stalled.md)). Keep chat as a means to the next meeting, not the product. |
| Newcomer barriers (theme 7) | 0 Onboarding, [open-gaps #37](../../workflow/open-gaps.md) | Supports targeting newcomers in their first 3 months. But don't position Firefly as newcomers-only, because locals whose friends moved away are users too. |
| Founders were lonely newcomers (theme 8) | H Host flow | First outreach list: Lumi, We Should Be Friends, Mondays Run Club, VanCity Girls Who Walk, Let's Adventure Vancouver ([organizers.md](../organizers.md)). |
