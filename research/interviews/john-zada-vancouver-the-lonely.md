## Quotes

> "What struck me right away is the degree of social awkwardness." — **Kate Goddard**, 35, moved here in 2022

> "Even at a concert here, no one talks to each other." — **Kate Goddard**

> "Daily life presents less challenges here. The best friendships, in my opinion, are born out of mutual bonds of struggle." — **Kate Goddard**

> "It's the most uptight 'laid back city' I've ever known… and definitely the hardest place I've experienced to try and make friends." — **Psychologist** who left Vancouver for Quebec

> "'I'll leave you alone—if you leave me alone' is the overall vibe." — **Unnamed friend**, still lives in Vancouver

> "I've lived in six countries on four continents. And it's clear that even compared to London, Vancouver is disengaged." — **David Beattie** (*The Guardian*, 2017)

> "People don't call you back, people don't invite you out, they don't make eye contact." — **David Beattie** (*The Guardian*, 2017)

> "There's a perception here that making and maintaining friendships requires intense effort and sacrifice" — **Robin Esrock**, Vancouver author (blog)

> "Not returning emails or phone calls, not inviting people to houses" — **Robin Esrock**, listing local habits

## Takeaways

- The most recent first-hand newcomer account in this folder (2026) matches the 2012–2025 accounts. **The freeze hasn't thawed.**
- **"Leave me alone if I leave you alone"** describes a norm, not a personality type. Nobody makes the first move, so nobody gets invited.
- Not following up ("don't call you back," "don't invite you out") is named again and again. It is the same follow-through gap as Zetta's funnel ([daily-hive-zetta-friend-tracking.md](daily-hive-zetta-friend-tracking.md)).
- Goddard's "bonds of struggle" idea: shared challenges (a race, a project, a volunteer shift) may bond people faster than casual mixers.
- Caveat: this is an opinion essay. Zada himself says how lonely a place is "is difficult to quantify and prove."
