## Quotes

> "I was convinced I'd be fine. Unfortunately, I was wrong." — **Katrina Martin**, on moving here

> "I got a lot of comments from people saying they'd lived here for years and still had no friends." — **Katrina Martin**, on responses to her TikTok

> "Some even said they had to leave because it was so hard to meet people." — **Katrina Martin**

> "Within half an hour, the nerves go away when you realize everyone else here came alone too." — **Katrina Martin**

> "Society has started to romanticize social isolation. It sounds nice until you realize—no, we actually do need people." — **Katrina Martin**

> "If you meet someone you connect with, don't just swap numbers—make a plan to hang out within the next two weeks." — **Katrina Martin**

> "I always say adult friendships take a lot of time and intention… You need to keep showing up, again and again. And you need to prioritize it." — **Katrina Martin**

> "I just got a DM from someone saying they're now an auntie to a baby because they met their best friend through book club." — **Katrina Martin**

## Numbers

- First meetup at Kits Beach: **100+** people turned up.

## Takeaways

- Huge latent demand: one TikTok → 100+ strangers at a beach.
- Loneliness is bad enough that some people **leave the city**.
- "Everyone came alone" is what makes a first event feel safe.
- The founder's own advice is the product thesis: **don't just swap numbers, set the next plan within two weeks**, and keep showing up. The friendship that stuck came from a **recurring** format (book club).
